# TSA 27 counterfactual synthesis

**Status:** evidence synthesis, 29 October 2026.
**Question:** what explains the AAC-to-harvest ratio since 2017, and how much is caribou?

## Short answer

The ratio did not decline smoothly from 2017. It rebounded in 2018, collapsed in 2019–2021,
recovered in 2022, dipped in 2023, and recovered strongly in 2025.

The evidence supports a **layered** answer:

- **Caribou/UWR:** a real but bounded legal effect, plausibly **5–15% of AAC** from the
  TSR4/TSR2 sensitivities, with the 2019 UWR expansion as the clearest timing event.
- **Fire:** too small at TSA scale to explain the aggregate gap; a local operating and salvage
  issue.
- **Access and road cost:** a spatial constraint and a margin conditioner, not a sudden
  aggregate collapse.
- **Mill volume capacity:** not binding at design capacity; even one mill is above AAC.
- **Price:** explains part of 2019 and most of 2025, but not 2021–2023.
- **Processing, mill demand, and undercut:** the leading residual for 2019–2023.

## Layer-by-layer evidence

### Caribou and UWR

| Evidence | Result | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| TSR4 2010 incremental caribou reserves | +8.4% short/mid, +2.2% long-term | Published sensitivity, not a TSA 27 run |
| TSR4 2010 caribou-LU biodiversity | +4.0% short/mid, +2.5% long-term | Published sensitivity |
| TSR2 1999 no caribou guidelines | +8.2% long-term | Analog |
| S7 vs S8 even-flow | 1.58–1.74M vs 2.21–2.44M m³ per 10-year period | Whole-UWR capacity, not AAC |
| 2019 UWR u-3-005 expansion | +10,601 ha (+10.0%); 0.841M m³ positive pool removed (9.8%) | Reduced-form add-back, not a WS4 recompile |

The 2019 expansion coincides with the drop from 92.8% to 49.1% of AAC. The size is real but
bounded. The S7/S8 gap is a capacity comparison, not a 29% AAC effect.

### Fire

| Evidence | Result |
|---|---|
| Peak fire/block overlap | 1,583 ha in 2018 (1.65% of operable area); 1,470 ha in 2023 (1.53%) |
| Peak operable volume at risk | 99,338 m³ in 2023 (0.41% of operable pool) |
| Fire with no salvage in the UWR pool | At most 47,385 m³ in a year, under 0.6% of the positive pool |

Fire cannot explain the aggregate gap. It matters for local operating areas, salvage windows,
and access.

### Access and road cost

| Evidence | Result |
|---|---|
| Shared-road screen | 52.6% full / 56.9% UWR positive at central costs |
| Price −20% | 4.7% / 5.0% positive |
| Cost +20% | 8.3% / 9.8% positive |
| Long-spur tail | P90 road cost about 69 $/m³ for blocks above 100 m³ |
| Active FTEN road length | 2,381 km in 2017 to 2,194 km in 2026 (−7.9%) |

Access is a spatial constraint and a margin conditioner. It does not show a sudden
2019–2021 collapse.

### Mill capacity

| Evidence | Result |
|---|---|
| Both design mills | 707,292 m³/yr, 3.14× AAC |
| Downie only | 471,528 m³/yr, 2.10× AAC |
| One-mill pilot | 66,439 m³, 0.748M net vs 65,206 m³, 0.678M two mills |
| Eagle River status | Absent from 2019/2022/2023 major-mill lists; unverified |

Mill **volume** capacity is not binding in any single-mill design case. Product acceptance,
grade mix, recovery, and current operating shifts are the remaining mill constraints.

### Price, stumpage, and the public-data envelope

A public-data envelope combines the BC interior AMV with five stumpage anchors from 12.76 to
55.22 $/m³. It shows which conclusions survive the full public range:

| Year | HBS / AAC | Positive-share range | Read |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 2019 | 49.1% | 0.0–47.9% | Can fit at low stumpage; not identified |
| 2021 | 30.2% | **77.8–96.2%** | Quantity/processing constraint is robust |
| 2022 | 55.0% | **67.1–95.6%** | Quantity/processing constraint is robust |
| 2025 | 83.4% | 0.0–85.2% | Can fit at low stumpage; not identified |

The 2021–2022 result is the strongest evidence in the model. Even the worst nested corner
(highest public stumpage, cost +20%, fire no-salvage) leaves 77.8% and 67.1% positive against
30.2% and 55.0% actual harvest.

BC interior lumber AMV price-only screen:

| Year | HBS / AAC | AMV factor | Positive share |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2019 | 49.1% | 0.878 | 21.1% |
| 2021 | 30.2% | 1.978 | 95.4% |
| 2022 | 55.0% | 1.876 | 94.2% |
| 2023 | 42.5% | 1.167 | 79.2% |
| 2025 | 83.4% | 1.224 | 81.5% |

The AMV fits 2019 and 2025 directionally at low stumpage. It fails for 2021–2023 even at the
highest public stumpage anchor: high prices and high positive margins coincide with low
harvest. That is a quantity/processing signal.

### Cutblock area and harvest intensity

| Evidence | Result |
|---|---|
| Cutblock area 2019–2021 | 1,707 ha, only 8% below 2017–2018 |
| HBS 2019–2021 vs 2017–2018 | −24.3% |
| 2025 cutblock area | 476 ha, less than half of 2018 |
| 2025 HBS | 83.4% of AAC |
| Cutblock ha per 1,000 m³ | 7.34 in 2020, 6.76 in 2021, 2.54 in 2025 |

The land base was not the binding constraint in 2025. The trough harvested more area per unit
volume, consistent with lower-volume or partial stands.

## Bounded attribution

These ranges are **not additive**. They describe what each layer can explain on its own
evidence.

| Layer | Plausible share of the AAC-to-harvest gap | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Caribou/UWR policy | **5–15% of AAC**; 2019 step is the clear timing event | Medium |
| Fire | **<1% of annual operable volume**; local effects larger | High at TSA scale |
| Access/road cost | Conditions **5–10%** of the positive pool; long-spur tail | Medium |
| Mill volume capacity | **≈0** at design capacity; one mill still above AAC | High |
| Price/market | Explains part of 2019 and most of 2025; not 2021–2023 | Medium |
| Processing/mill demand/undercut | **Leading residual**, especially 2019–2023 | Medium-high |

The 2019 low is the one year where price and caribou timing align. The 2021 low is the
clearest quantity/processing signal. The 2025 recovery is consistent with market and
processing recovery, not with a larger land base.

## What would change the answer

1. A TSA 27 **billed-stumpage** series, not the zone-average table.
2. Dated **mill curtailment and restart** events for Downie/Selkirk and the regional cluster.
3. A full **WS4 recompile** with the 2019 UWR expansion removed.
4. Burn severity, salvage, and post-fire recovery.
5. A nested counterfactual that varies caribou, fire, access, mill capacity, and price
   together rather than one at a time.
