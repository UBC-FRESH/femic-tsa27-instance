TSA 27 Revelstoke — executive brief

Date: 29 October 2026

Question: why has scaled harvest fallen below the AAC since 2017, and how much is caribou?


ANSWER

The decline is not a single land-base effect.

The ratio fell sharply in 2019–2021, recovered in 2022, dipped in 2023, and recovered strongly in 2025.

The evidence supports four layers:

• Caribou/UWR is a real but bounded legal effect, plausibly 5–15% of AAC. The 2019 u-3-005 expansion (+10,601 ha) is the clearest timing event.

• Fire is small at TSA scale, under 1% of annual operable volume, though it matters locally through salvage and access.

• Access and road cost condition margins, especially for long-spur blocks, but do not show a sudden aggregate collapse.

• The dominant residual is undercut / processing / mill demand, especially in 2019–2023.


OBSERVED PATH

WHAT THE PUBLIC-DATA MODEL SUPPORTS

Caribou/UWR policy:
5–15% of AAC. The 2019 step removes 0.841M m³ of positive-margin volume, 9.8% of the pre-2019 pool.
Confidence: medium.

Fire no-salvage:
Under 0.6% of the positive pool in a year.
Confidence: high at TSA scale.

Access/road cost:
Conditions 5–10% of the positive pool.
Confidence: medium.

Mill volume capacity:
About zero. Downie alone is 2.10× AAC.
Confidence: high.

2021–2022:
Quantity/processing constraint is robust.
2021 positive share is 77.8–96.2% against 30.2% actual.
2022 positive share is 67.1–95.6% against 55.0% actual.
Confidence: high.

2019 and 2025:
Range-dependent. Not uniquely identified without TSA billed stumpage.
Confidence: low–medium.


THE THREE KEY YEARS

2019.
UWR u-3-005 expands by 10,601 ha, the price index falls, and harvest drops from 92.8% to 49.1%.
This is the one year where price and caribou timing align.
The exact split is not identified because the public stumpage range spans the observed harvest.

2021.
Lumber prices are at their maximum, but harvest is the lowest in the series at 30.2%.
Even at the highest public stumpage and with cost +20% and fire no-salvage, the positive-margin share is 77.8%.
The constraint is on the quantity side: mill demand, processing, contractors, or operational access.

2025.
Harvest recovers to 83.4% on a smaller cutblock area and a weaker price year than 2021.
That rules out a simple land-base explanation.
The recovery is consistent with stronger mill demand/processing and a lower realized stumpage, but the public data do not identify the split.


WHAT THIS MEANS

• Do not treat the AAC-to-harvest gap as a caribou netdown. Caribou is a bounded legal effect.

• Do not treat fire as the aggregate driver. It is a local salvage and access issue.

• Mill volume capacity is not the constraint. Product acceptance, grade mix, and operating shifts are the remaining mill questions.

• The 2021–2022 trough is the strongest evidence for a quantity/processing explanation.

• The 2019 and 2025 attributions should be reported as ranges until a TSA-specific billed stumpage series is available.
